When a high-mountain slope failure cascades downstream: reconstructing the 26 August 2026 Gyirong mixed rock–ice disaster
Abstract
On 26 August 2026, a high-elevation slope failure involving rock and glacier ice near the China–Nepal border developed into a channelised mass-flow and flood-affected cascade that reached Gyirong Port/Timure. We reconstructed this source–pathway–receptor system from open and publicly released Earth-observation records, combining event-bracketing Sentinel-2, Landsat-9, PlanetScope and Sentinel-1 observations, Copernicus DEM terrain analysis, and UNOSAT and Copernicus Emergency Management Service rapid mapping. Nested multisensor envelopes constrained the post-event changed/source surface to a preferred area of 1.009 km
2
(conservative–inclusive range 0.491–1.841 km
2
). A terrain- and drainage-constrained representative route extended 21.843 km in plan to Gyirong Port and descended approximately 3396 m; it is a corridor descriptor, not a particle trajectory. The prespecified pre-event Sentinel-2 sequence showed no unambiguous large-scale surface change, and the final usable optical observation preceded failure by 45.85 h. Neither GPM IMERG V07 Late nor ERA5-Land placed any of five prespecified precipitation windows in an unusually wet upper tail, although their amounts and historical ranks differed strongly. The 7 d ERA5-Land mean 2 m temperature (9.43
∘
C) and positive-degree-day total (65.94
∘
C d) ranked above all 25 matched years from 2001–2025. These gridded values provide regional thermal context and do not identify a trigger. Longer-term records support anthropogenic influence on regional summer climate and growth of downstream built-up exposure, while any mechanical link to the rupture remains unresolved. Independent rapid mapping delineated a 37.353 km
2
downstream disturbed footprint and contained 695 building points and 15.671 km of roads graded destroyed; these are preliminary mapped features rather than a complete damage census. The principal contribution is an uncertainty-explicit, reproducible reconstruction of the full cascade and its observation-scale limits: open Earth observation recovered robust first-order source, propagation, environmental-context and impact constraints, while the exact detached perimeter, source volume, short-lived pre-failure activity, initiation mechanics and source-scale hydrology remained unresolved.

\introduction
High-mountain rock–ice cascades are source–pathway–receptor systems. A failure involving bedrock, glacier ice, seasonal snow or unconsolidated sediment may fragment, entrain valley-floor material, enter a confined drainage, impound or displace water, and transform into a debris flow or sediment-laden flood. The resulting downstream footprint can greatly exceed the initial source area
(
Schneider et al., 2011
;
Mani et al., 2023
;
Zhong et al., 2025
)
. Understanding such an event therefore requires more than mapping a scar: the source, propagation corridor, process transitions and affected receptors must be reconstructed together.
Complete reconstruction is difficult because the components are observed by different systems and at different times. Source areas are remote and steep; cloud, seasonal snow and terrain shadow obscure optical imagery; radar responses are affected by viewing geometry and surface state; and rapid-response maps record selected consequences rather than a complete process history. Field access may also be delayed, and a single cascade can cross administrative or national boundaries. These conditions make uncertainty representation and separation of direct observation from interpretation central scientific requirements rather than secondary reporting details.
Recent events demonstrate the diversity of source conditions and cascade evolution. The 2016 Aru events were low-angle glacier detachments associated with surge-like instability, whereas repeated Flat Creek detachments involved a dynamically constrained glacier with a soft bed and high meltwater availability
(
TIAN et al., 2017
;
Kääb et al., 2018
;
Gilbert et al., 2018
;
Jacquemart et al., 2020
)
. At Chamoli in 2021, steep bedrock and glacier ice fragmented and entrained material before evolving into a destructive debris flow; later work also documented long-term motion and cracking
(
Shugar et al., 2021
;
de Vries et al., 2022
)
. Repeated Sedongpu events show how glacier and rock–ice failures can produce exceptional erosion, river blockage and landscape reorganisation
(
An et al., 2022
;
Zhao et al., 2022
;
Kääb and Girod, 2023
;
Li et al., 2024
;
Zhang et al., 2025
)
. Together, these cases show that source type, entrainment, process transformation and downstream effects vary substantially, and that glacier collapse, rock avalanche, glacier detachment and glacial-lake outburst flood are not interchangeable labels.
Environmental context, source preconditioning and immediate triggering also require different evidence. Permafrost degradation, glacier retreat, extreme warmth and liquid water are physically relevant to high-mountain slope stability, but their roles depend on local lithology, fracture structure, thermal state and hydrology, which are seldom observed directly
(
Gruber et al., 2004
;
Huggel, 2009
;
Huggel et al., 2010b
;
Huggel et al., 2010a
;
Gruber et al., 2017
)
. An environmental anomaly can be consistent with preconditioning without identifying the immediate trigger. Likewise, regional anthropogenic climate attribution does not establish event attribution, and historical development can increase exposure without amplifying hazard magnitude. These distinctions are essential when regional warming, unresolved source mechanics and changing settlement patterns coexist
(
Kargel et al., 2016
;
Fujita et al., 2017
;
Zhuang et al., 2024
;
Jiang and Zhou, 2023
;
Pesaresi et al., 2024
)
.
Multi-source Earth observation can nevertheless recover complementary parts of an inaccessible cascade. Optical imagery can locate changed surfaces and map snow, ice, debris and channel disturbance; radar amplitude can provide an independent all-weather change signal; digital elevation models can constrain source and route geometry; reanalysis and satellite precipitation can describe regional environmental conditions; and rapid maps can document selected downstream effects. Pre-failure surface motion or morphological change is one additional observational dimension, but its interpretation depends on sensor resolution, cadence, cloud, snow, texture and terrain geometry. The value of a multisensor reconstruction is therefore greatest when direct observations, derived quantities, supported interpretations and unresolved mechanisms remain explicit
(
Huggel et al., 2005
;
Shugar et al., 2021
;
Zhao et al., 2022
;
Kirschbaum et al., 2019
)
.
For Gyirong,
Guo et al. (2026)
provide the event-specific process benchmark. They reconstructed the source-to-port chain and progressive entrainment using GF-1/GF-7 and other satellite imagery, field photographs, KKN seismic records, monitoring video, eyewitness constraints and a dedicated 2024–2026 Sentinel-2 glacier-velocity analysis. Their study reported limited source precipitation during the final 72 h, anomalously warm seasonal conditions, relatively high surface velocities in part of the source glacier and candidate seismic signals during the hours before the main event, without identifying a single immediate trigger. This evidential scope provides a process-rich reference against which an independent open-data reconstruction can be compared.
Here we present an uncertainty-aware reconstruction of the full Gyirong source–pathway–receptor cascade using open and publicly released Earth-observation data. The GF imagery, field photographs, seismic waveforms, video and autoRIFT/TICOI velocity results of
Guo et al. (2026)
were not used to define our measurements and served only as an independent comparison. This design permits cross-validation of first-order geometry and source classification while treating source-perimeter uncertainty, prespecified-window optical visibility limits, hydroclimatic product sensitivity, rapid-map damage grades and exposure intersections as distinct evidence classes.
We address three integrated questions: (1) what source geometry, propagation route and downstream consequences can be reconstructed and quantified while representing uncertainty; (2) what pre-event environmental and surface-observation conditions are supported, including hydroclimate, long-term regional context and the limits of routine optical detectability; and (3) what does the Gyirong event reveal about the capabilities and limits of open Earth observation for reconstructing rapidly evolving high-mountain hazard chains? The study does not perform formal event attribution or dynamic runout simulation, and pre-event observability is one component of the event reconstruction rather than its organizing question.
1
Study area and event
The mapped source is on the northern flank of the Langtang Lirung massif near the China–Nepal border, in steep glacierised terrain draining northward towards the Gyirong/Timure border corridor (Fig.
1
). The preferred source centroid is 85.52701
∘
E, 28.28858
∘
N. Regional relief is high: the preferred source reaches 5221 m, whereas Gyirong Port lies near 1823 m. The upper route is steep and confined; farther downstream the drainage intersects settlements, roads, border facilities and the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli corridor. The area was also affected by snow and ice avalanches during the 2015 Gorkha earthquake, but the 2026 event was not classified here as earthquake-triggered
(
Kargel et al., 2016
)
.
We use 26 August 2026 at 02:52:00 UTC (10:52 Beijing time) as the reference event time, corresponding to the reported mass-movement signal. An official account gives an approximate time near 10:30 Beijing time, and another signal time is 20 s later; these differences define the timing uncertainty
(
Jiangsu Earthquake Agency, 2026
;
China Geological Survey, 2026
)
. All acquisition offsets and pre/post classifications use exact UTC timestamps rather than calendar dates.
Initial public descriptions variously used glacier collapse, ice avalanche, landslide, mudslide and flood. The China Geological Survey’s preliminary assessment described a high-elevation ice collapse that entrained moraine material and developed into a debris flow
(
China Geological Survey, 2026
)
. We delineated the source independently before comparing it with these external descriptions. Place names are used descriptively and no boundary line is intended to express a jurisdictional position.

2
Data and methods
2.1
Analysis scope, evidence classes and event clock
We reconstructed the event retrospectively using post-event multisensor observations to identify the source, route and downstream impacts. Source identification was based on convergent optical and radar evidence across plausible headwalls in the affected drainage; the reported source geometry was defined independently from earlier exploratory source-location attempts. To limit overinterpretation, we distinguish direct observations, derived measurements, supported process interpretations and unresolved questions throughout the analysis. Direct observations are visible features or features in independent mapping products; derived measurements are reproducible quantities calculated from defined data and geometries; supported interpretations connect multiple observations through process reasoning; and unresolved questions are not constrained by the available data. The accompanying reproducibility materials link the principal claims to their evidence and limitations.
The event time in Sect.
1
was applied before calculating all acquisition offsets. Images ending strictly before 02:52:00 UTC were pre-event; images starting afterward were post-event. No same-day acquisition was classified from date alone.

2.3
Source mapping and classification
The source search covered all plausible headwalls around the candidate drainage and did not begin from a scar-shaped region. Pre- and post-event optical images were compared on common grids. Mapping cues were a new fresh or scoured surface, disappearance or reorganisation of snow/ice, exposed darker rock or debris, headwall change and spatial continuity with the downstream affected corridor. Radar amplitude change was used as supporting convergence only because snow state, wetness and geometry can also change amplitude.
Three nested polygons express interpretive uncertainty in the perimeter of the mapped post-event changed/source surface. The conservative envelope contains only the fresh/scoured surface, snow/ice loss or reorganisation, and exposed rock/debris supported most consistently across visible observations. The preferred envelope represents the connected changed/source surface best supported by the combined observations. The inclusive envelope additionally contains plausible edges obscured or rendered ambiguous by cloud, haze, shadow and mixed surface change. These polygons do not represent measured failure depth, volume, rock/ice fractions or probabilistic confidence intervals, and they need not be identical to the initially detached body. The source geometry was finalized before comparison with external source locations. The source term was then selected from glacier detachment, hanging-glacier failure, ice avalanche, rock avalanche, mixed rock–ice slope failure or unresolved, with composition and initial kinematics treated separately.
2.4
Terrain and process-chain reconstruction
Source metrics were calculated in UTM zone 45N. Elevation, slope and aspect were sampled on a 10 m analysis grid derived from Copernicus DEM GLO-30. Aspect distribution used eight 45
∘

A representative centreline connected the source to Gyirong Port while remaining within the independent UNOSAT affected footprint, except for a 15 m half-pixel support buffer. DEM and mapped waterways guided the centreline. 2.5
Pre-event optical observations
Six Sentinel-2 windows were selected before visual interpretation to represent approximately one year, six months, three months, one month, two weeks and the latest usable observation before failure. Within each target window, selection prioritized source-local scene-classification usability and then proximity to the nominal date; no date was chosen for apparent anomaly. All panels use a common projection, extent and comparable reflectance display. Interpretation was restricted to large-scale change resolvable at 10 m. We separately tested a feature-tracking configuration defined before inspecting the source results, but no source displacement estimate met both the stable-terrain-error and spatial-coherence requirements; displacement was therefore excluded from the main results.
2.6
Radar change
Sentinel-1 VV and VH radiometrically terrain-corrected amplitude observations bracketing the event were co-registered on the event grid and compared in logarithmic space. We interpreted only spatially coincident source or corridor changes supported by optical or rapid-map evidence. Conventional InSAR was not attempted because steep topography, layover/shadow, snow/ice decorrelation and potentially non-phase-resolvable displacement made a robust phase result unlikely. Coherence and amplitude-change methods can assist rapid landslide mapping, but are non-unique and must be checked against independent information
(
Burrows et al., 2020
;
Jacquemart and Tiampo, 2021
)
.

2.8
Historical environmental and exposure context
Longer-term July–August temperature was evaluated using the same predefined spatial supports and seasonal definition. Hourly ERA5-Land data for 1951–2025 were reduced to annual July–August means at the source-nearest grid cell and over the fixed Central Himalaya regional support. We calculated ordinary-least-squares (OLS) trends with 95 % confidence intervals, Theil–Sen slopes, Mann–Kendall statistics, and 10- and 20-year running means for 1951–2025, 1976–2025 and 2001–2025. The extended record was validated by reproducing the previously calculated 2001–2025 trends before analysis of the longer time series
(
Muñoz-Sabater et al., 2021
)
.
Berkeley Earth monthly land temperature over the same regional box, where available, supplied an independent July–August sensitivity record for 1951–2024
(
Rohde et al., 2013
;
Rohde and Hausfather, 2020
)
. CMIP6/DAMIP forcing consistency used four prespecified models with paired historical/all-forcing and historical-natural simulations, one member per model and equal model weight, over the same regional box. Comparisons were restricted to July–August 1951–2020 and 1971–2020 because the observational record used here is seasonal rather than full-year. This comparison tests whether the observed trend lies more consistently within the small all-forcing or natural-only model distributions; it is not optimal fingerprinting, formal event attribution or an estimate of the anthropogenic fraction of warming
(
Gillett et al., 2016
)
.
Six historical source-image epochs (1972, 1987, 2000, 2008, 2020 and 2026) were inspected within a fixed source-context region. Cross-sensor interpretation was harmonised to a 120 m minimum mapping unit. Because seasonal snow, terrain shadow and sensor differences prevented like-for-like delineation, we did not estimate a quantitative source-cryosphere trend. We also examined the images for direct surface disturbance within fixed 0.5, 1, 2 and 5 km source buffers. Historical built-up exposure was measured independently with GHSL R2023A built-up-surface grids for 1975, 1990, 2000, 2015 and 2020 in four fixed zones: the UNOSAT affected footprint, Timure/Gyirong Port, Syapru Besi and a fixed 500 m corridor buffer. Built-up area and fraction were calculated at nominal zone geometry and with low/high boundary-sampling sensitivity; they measure exposure, not population, damage, casualties or hazard magnitude
(
Pesaresi et al., 2024
)
.
2.9
Channel change, damage and exposure
Twelve cross-valley transects were placed at terrain- and channel-defined positions before widths were inspected. At each transect we separately attempted to measure a channel-connected pre/post water segment and the width of the independent mapped disturbed footprint. A water width was reported only when both dates met the prespecified valid-pixel and connectivity rule. Disturbed width therefore does not imply flowing-water width. Its reported uncertainty contains a
±
20
\pm 20
m pixel-edge component but not a formal interpreter-boundary term.
Copernicus damage grades were summarized by delivered feature class and area of interest. Road lengths were calculated from mapped line geometry; buildings, transport points and facilities were counted as mapped features. OpenStreetMap roads and bridge-tagged features were intersected with the UNOSAT footprint to characterize exposure only. No casualty or population estimate was inferred, and rapid-map counts were not treated as complete inventories.

Results
3.1
Source identification and geometry
The final pre-event Sentinel-2 image was acquired at 05:00:49 UTC on 24 August, 45.85 h before the failure clock. Post-event Landsat-9, Sentinel-2 and partly unobscured PlanetScope imagery showed a new fresh or scoured surface, loss or reorganisation of snow and ice, and exposure of darker debris or rock in the same high-elevation slope sector (Fig.
2
). Sentinel-1 RTC amplitude change between 16 and 28 August was spatially coincident with the optical change, although the radar response was not unique to mass movement. The Pelican and SkySat strips examined did not cover the mapped source; source-covering PlanetScope scenes were strongly affected by cloud and haze.

3.2
Source-to-downstream cascade
A representative centreline constrained by the independent UNOSAT footprint and mapped drainage connected the source to Gyirong Port/Timure (Fig.
4
). The planar source-to-port length was 21.843 km and the DEM-derived surface length was 22.710 km. The route descended from approximately 5220 m at the source crown to 1823 m at the port, an endpoint drop of 3396 m and a mean endpoint angle of 8.84
∘
(Fig.
5

Pre-event surface observations and observational limits

The independent UNOSAT footprint intersected all 12 terrain-positioned transects (Supplement). Disturbed-corridor widths ranged from 145 to 1298 m, with a median of 533 m. The nine source-to-port transects had a median width of 970 m, whereas the three downstream transects had a median width of 195 m. The

No transect yielded a defensible pre/post water-width pair under the prespecified valid-pixel and channel-connectivity rule. 4
Discussion
4.1
Source classification and uncertainty
Our strongest source evidence is the convergence of a new optical surface change, snow/ice loss or reorganisation, exposed darker rock/debris, steep topography and spatially coincident Sentinel-1 amplitude change. This combination makes
mixed rock–ice slope failure
more defensible than either glacier collapse or rock avalanche alone. Independent GF imagery and field photographs presented by
Guo et al. (2026)
show glacier ice and rock involvement and residual hanging ice, providing external consistency with this material classification. Those observations were not used to define our source geometry.
The two studies attach different areas to the source. Our preferred 1.009 km
2
value, bounded by conservative and inclusive mapping envelopes of 0.491–1.841 km
2
, describes the connected post-event changed/source surface visible across our optical and radar evidence.
Guo et al. (2026)
report an approximately 0.72 km
2
collapsed ice–rock body using GF and other event-specific observations. The estimates should not be treated as interchangeable or statistically combined: one is explicitly a changed-surface perimeter with obscured-edge envelopes, whereas the other is described as the collapsed source body. Differences in sensor resolution, observation dates, cloud and haze, inclusion of fresh/scoured margins, and manual perimeter interpretation are plausible non-exclusive explanations. Without both digital polygons on a common grid, their overlap and boundary displacement cannot be quantified.
The classification remains interpretive. Optical appearance cannot quantify rock-to-ice proportion, and the lack of a co-registered pre/post DEM prevents failure-depth and volume estimates. The source envelopes express visible perimeter ambiguity, not volumetric uncertainty. The mapped geometry also differs from the low-angle, soft-bed detachment settings documented at Aru and Flat Creek
(
Kääb et al., 2018
;
Jacquemart et al., 2020
;
Kääb et al., 2021
)
. Because neither our data nor the external consistency establishes whether rupture began in bedrock, glacier ice or their interface, a more specific initiation label would overstate the evidence.
No mapped evidence of large persistent direct anthropogenic surface disturbance was identified at the source within the observable historical record, subject to the stated resolution and visibility limits. This bounded non-detection is not proof that no human activity occurred: narrow, transient, subsurface, unmapped or terrain-shadowed disturbance could remain unresolved in the 1972–2026 images.
4.2
Cascade transformation and mobility
Continuity from the source through a steep confined valley to the independently mapped downstream footprint supports a channel-coupled cascade in which the initial mass fragmented and entered channelised transport. Our independent 21.843 km plan length, 3396 m endpoint drop and 8.84
∘
endpoint angle constrain the source-to-port geometry. They do not quantify rheology, velocity, discharge, entrained volume or the trajectory of individual particles. The terrain-based transition at 5.94 km is likewise an interpretive reach boundary.
Event-specific evidence for progressive erosion and entrainment is stronger in
Guo et al. (2026)
, who combine GF imagery, field observations and process information to infer bed and bank scour, incorporation of unconsolidated material, and interaction with channel water and sediment. Our mapped corridor is consistent with such amplification, as are analogues from Kolka, Chamoli and Sedongpu
(
Huggel et al., 2005
;
Shugar et al., 2021
;
Kääb and Girod, 2023
;
Zhang et al., 2025
)
, but our data neither isolate erosion and deposition volumes nor demonstrate how much mass was added. Accordingly, progressive entrainment is external event-specific support for the cascade interpretation, not an independently quantified result of this study. A dynamic model would add false precision without source volume, material fractions, hydrograph or suitable validation.
4.3
Environmental context and limits of causal inference
Our prespecified 3 d IMERG Late accumulation of 8.3 mm is numerically consistent with the approximately 8.3 mm source-area accumulation over the final 72 h reported independently by
Guo et al. (2026)
. This agreement supports limited immediate antecedent rainfall in that gridded product. It does not establish source-scale rainfall, because product version, cut-off convention, grid-cell selection and complex-orographic sampling still require care.
The broader five-window comparison adds information beyond that common 72 h value. Neither IMERG nor ERA5-Land placed any of the five prespecified event windows in an unusually wet upper tail, but the products disagreed strongly in amount and historical rank over 7–30 d. ERA5-Land indicated anomalously warm regional conditions: the 3 and 7 d mean temperatures and 7 d PDD ranked above all 25 matched 2001–2025 years, and an objectively defined five-day warm spell ended on 25 August. This is consistent with the warmer spring and summer conditions reported by
Guo et al. (2026)
, while our contribution is the matched-year ranking, prespecified windows, two-product comparison and explicit representativeness analysis.
The warm regional period is physically compatible with enhanced seasonal melt or thermal/hydrological preconditioning in ice-bearing terrain, but such preconditioning is not demonstrated here. The approximately 0.1
∘
products smooth complex orography, and the ERA5-Land grid elevation is 539 m below the preferred source median. PDD is a temperature-index proxy, not an energy-balance melt observation
(
Wake and Marshall, 2015
)
. No observation constrains source-scale meltwater production, water storage or routing, pore pressure, ice temperature, permafrost distribution, fracture ice or failure-plane hydrology. Permafrost conditions are particularly poorly known across the Hindu Kush Himalaya
(
Gruber et al., 2017
)
. Consequently, neither our analysis nor the external comparison proves or disproves a thermal, melt or rainfall trigger.
Regional July–August warming is supported by ERA5-Land and Berkeley Earth and is more consistent with CMIP6 simulations including anthropogenic forcing than with natural-only simulations. The agreement is strongest for the sign and multidecadal scale of regional summer warming; model spread, sparse topographic sampling and the seasonal observational target preclude a more specific attribution statement. This regional attribution does not establish that anthropogenic warming caused or mechanically contributed to the 2026 rupture. The source-specific links involving permafrost, meltwater, pore pressure, fracture weakening, ice or rock temperature and failure-plane hydrology remain unresolved. The independently observed short warm period is therefore regional environmental context, not evidence of a human-induced trigger or source-scale causal mechanism
(
Jiang and Zhou, 2023
)
.
4.4
Pre-event observability across time scales
The routine Sentinel-2 sequence showed no unambiguous large-scale pre-failure surface change, and the final usable optical observation preceded failure by 45.85 h. No corrected source-displacement estimate met the predefined stable-terrain-error and spatial-coherence criteria. These findings apply only to resolvable surface change in the six prespecified optical snapshots. They do not exclude persistent motion detectable through dense feature tracking, sub-pixel change, change obscured by cloud, shadow or snow, or activity within the final observation gap
(
Lacroix et al., 2018
;
Chudley et al., 2022
;
Zheng et al., 2023
;
Altena et al., 2022
)
.
The dedicated analysis of
Guo et al. (2026)
addresses a different observable. Their supplement describes autoRIFT displacement measurements from 211 Sentinel-2 scenes acquired between January 2024 and August 2026, 60–400 d image-pair baselines, stable-area correction, robust TICOI inversion and 15 d velocity output. They report relatively high 2024–2026 velocity in parts of the source glacier. A multi-image velocity inversion can recover persistent motion that is not visible as an unambiguous morphological change in six snapshots; our snapshot-based non-detection therefore neither reproduces nor refutes their velocity result. Reviews of avalanching-glacier instability likewise caution that velocity patterns are process-regime dependent and are not deterministic indicators by themselves
(
Faillettaz et al., 2015
;
Kääb et al., 2021
)
.
Guo et al. additionally identify several candidate KKN seismic signals during the approximately 3 h before the principal event and relate some timing to eyewitness or video observations, while declining to interpret them as deterministic indicators
(
Guo et al., 2026
)
. Those signals fall well inside our 45.85 h optical observation gap. The combined record therefore illustrates an observation-scale hierarchy: multi-year velocity and seasonal climate describe long-term state; days-to-weeks optical snapshots and thermal anomalies describe intermediate conditions; seismic or video records can sample activity over hours; and the main cascade propagates over minutes. Agreement or non-detection at one scale cannot be transferred automatically to another.
The source’s steep geometry and snow/ice cover also weaken a conventional InSAR interpretation. Layover, shadow, decorrelation and phase ambiguity may suppress or distort deformation, while amplitude change is non-unique. We therefore retain Sentinel-1 only as post-event amplitude-change support and do not claim a radar-derived deformation history.
4.5
Downstream consequences and changing exposure
The observed consequence extended far beyond the source. Independent rapid maps show a broad disturbed corridor and mapped damage at Timure/Gyirong Port and Syapru Besi. The 695 building points and 15.671 km of road graded destroyed summarize the published Copernicus EMS products, but their denominator, omission rate and field accuracy are unknown. The UNOSAT polygon and transect intersections describe a mapped affected corridor, not instantaneous flow width, water width, deposition area or inundation depth.
This impact emphasis is complementary to the process-amplification focus of
Guo et al. (2026)
. OpenStreetMap intersections answer which mapped assets lie within the rapid-map footprint, not which assets were damaged. Separating imagery-based change mapped in this study, independent rapid-map damage grades and exposure intersections avoids converting incomplete inventories into loss rates or casualty estimates. Water-width change could not be measured because cloud-obscured baselines and mixed pixels prevented channel-connected segments from meeting the prespecified criteria; disturbed widths therefore cannot be recast as flood-water widths.
Historical GHSL data independently show substantial growth of downstream built-up exposure. This represents anthropogenic amplification of exposure, not amplification of hazard magnitude or of the source failure itself. Built-up surface is not a complete measure of population, occupancy, asset value or vulnerability; it demonstrates only that more mapped development was situated within the fixed downstream zones by 2020
(
Pesaresi et al., 2024
)
.
4.6
What open Earth observation can and cannot reconstruct
The comparison shows that observing systems should be combined without being conflated. High-resolution forensic imagery and field photographs constrain source composition and erosion evidence; multi-year feature tracking constrains glacier motion; seismic, infrasound or video can sample short-lived activity; reanalysis and satellite precipitation describe regional environmental context; and rapid maps capture selected downstream consequences. Reviews of High Mountain Asia similarly identify spatial resolution, cloud, terrain, acquisition timing and incomplete end-to-end process observations as central limitations
(
Kirschbaum et al., 2019
)
.
Our open-data reconstruction recovers first-order source location and changed-surface uncertainty, route geometry, matched hydroclimatic context and operationally mapped impacts from data that can be independently re-accessed or traced. It also identifies hard limits: open optical snapshots did not resolve the hours-scale activity described externally; radar amplitude did not yield a deformation mechanism; regional climate did not resolve source hydrology; and rapid mapping did not produce a complete field inventory. The absence of unambiguous large-scale change in the prespecified optical sequence is therefore compatible with higher-frequency seismic activity, persistent motion derived from a denser image archive, or changes below the spatial or temporal resolution of the observations.
4.7
Limitations
The principal limitations are: (1) cloud and haze in all early source-covering post-event optical observations; (2) mapped-source envelopes informed by convergent multisensor evidence but not a second independent delineation; (3) no appropriate pre/post DEM and therefore no source, erosion or deposition volume; (4) a representative centreline and smoothed 30 m DEM profile rather than observed particle trajectories; (5) non-specific Sentinel-1 amplitude change and no validated source displacement; (6) a 45.85 h final optical gap; (7) gridded hydroclimate at approximately 0.1
∘
and an elevation mismatch; (8) inability to separate water width and disturbed width; (9) operational rapid maps without field validation or complete-census denominators; and (10) unresolved temporary blockage, material fractions and initiation mechanics.
A contemporaneous study also uses GF imagery, field photography, KKN seismic records, surveillance video, eyewitness timing and a dedicated Sentinel-2 velocity inversion that were not included in our analysis
(
Guo et al., 2026
)
. Our conclusions therefore concern the constraints available from the analysed open and publicly released record. This narrower evidential scope limits direct statements about entrainment, source composition and short-lived pre-failure activity, while retaining independently reproducible measurements of source change, first-order route geometry, regional climate ranks and mapped impacts.
\conclusions
An independent reconstruction from open and publicly released Earth-observation records recovers the first-order source, cascade geometry, environmental context and downstream consequences of the 26 August 2026 Gyirong event. The preferred post-event changed/source surface is 1.009 km
2
within conservative–inclusive mapping envelopes of 0.491–1.841 km
2
. A representative source-to-port route extends 21.843 km in plan and descends approximately 3396 m. These values agree in scale with a contemporaneous event-specific reconstruction, while the source-area estimates remain definition- and sensor-dependent and should not be merged. The open record supports classification as a mixed rock–ice slope failure followed by a channel-coupled mass-flow/flood cascade, but it does not independently quantify progressive entrainment, failure volume, rheology or a temporary blockage. Event-specific field, high-resolution, seismic and velocity evidence published elsewhere provides stronger constraints on some of those processes. Our separation of external evidence from analytical inputs preserves the independence and reproducibility of the present results.
The prespecified pre-event Sentinel-2 sequence showed no unambiguous large-scale surface change, and the final usable optical observation preceded failure by 45.85 h. This finding is limited by 10 m sampling and variable cloud, snow and illumination; it neither demonstrates stability nor excludes persistent motion, sub-pixel or obscured change, or hours-scale activity recoverable with denser or higher-frequency observations. Neither gridded precipitation product indicated an unusually wet antecedent period within the five prespecified windows. Regional thermal conditions were unusually warm: the 7 d ERA5-Land mean temperature and PDD ranked above all matched 2001–2025 years. These climate observations provide environmental context, not causal attribution. Independent rapid mapping delineated a 37.353 km
2
downstream disturbed footprint and contained 695 building points and 15.671 km of roads graded destroyed, but the layers are preliminary rather than a complete census. Historical GHSL data independently demonstrate substantial growth of mapped built-up exposure in the affected downstream zones, while the source-image record is insufficient for a quantitative multidecadal source-cryosphere trend. The present evidence supports anthropogenic influence on regional climate and substantial growth of downstream built-up exposure, but it does not establish a direct anthropogenic cause of the 2026 Gyirong slope failure.
The transferable conclusion is that open Earth observation can reconstruct the first-order source–pathway–receptor chain of a rapidly evolving high-mountain cascade while making observation-scale limits explicit. Regional anthropogenic climate influence is not event attribution; non-detection of large persistent local disturbance is not proof that no human activity occurred; lack of visible change in the prespecified optical sequence is not evidence of no pre-failure motion; and historical exposure growth is not hazard amplification. Source perimeters, short-lived activity and causal mechanisms remain dependent on sensor definition, cadence and complementary field or geophysical evidence.

Supplementary data provenance and quality control
The supplementary tables point to the versioned, machine-readable project outputs rather than reproducing the underlying satellite data. Scene identifiers, acquisition times, processing levels, quality masks and access restrictions are recorded in the acquisition and evidence ledgers. All internal time comparisons use UTC and the frozen reference time of 26 August 2026 at 02:52 UTC. The Planet catalogue was re-queried on 3 September 2026. It contained three post-event Pelican acquisitions from 1 September that were not present in the frozen analysed scene set; these later catalogue additions were documented but were not introduced into the analysis.
The mapped changed/source-surface envelopes are stored with the machine-readable event-report vectors under the following file name:
source_detachment_uncertainty_bounds.geojson
The integrated process evidence and representative centreline are stored separately.
File checksums and the protocol-amendment log preserve the distinction between the unsuccessful Phase 3A candidate screen and the final multisensor forensic source mapping. Planet imagery is not redistributed in this package.

Variable snow, shadow and cross-sensor resolution prevent a quantitative source-cryosphere trend.
The same historical images were audited for road cuts, mines or quarries, excavation, buildings, hydropower infrastructure, artificial terraces and persistent disturbed ground within fixed 0.5, 1, 2 and 5 km buffers. No mapped evidence of large persistent anthropogenic surface disturbance was identified within the observable historical record. This statement is bounded by the approximately 120 m persistent-feature mapping unit, the approximately 20 m sensitivity of the corrected 2026 Sentinel-2 view, terrain shadow and intermittent source visibility; it does not exclude small, temporary, subsurface, narrow or unmapped activity.

7
Damage, exposure and evidence classes
Copernicus EMSR927 records 323 and 372 building points graded destroyed in its two analysed areas of interest, respectively, for a delivered-layer total of 695. Roads graded destroyed total 6.148 and 9.523 km, respectively (15.671 km combined). The same layers include other damage grades, transport points and facility polygons. UNOSAT delineates a 37.354 km
2
affected footprint. These operational layers have neither a complete-census denominator nor field validation in this analysis.
OpenStreetMap intersections are exposure indicators: 7.414 km of mapped roads and two bridge-tagged features intersect the UNOSAT footprint. OSM segmentation and completeness affect these values. They are not independent confirmations of damage. Building-footprint and population exposure were not estimated, and no casualty inference was made.
